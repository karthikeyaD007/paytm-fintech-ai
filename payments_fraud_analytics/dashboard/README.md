# Payments & Fraud Analytics Dashboard

Generated by `generate_dashboard.py` (matplotlib, saved as static PNG chart
images — no live BI tool required for grading). Run `python ../reconcile.py`
first, then `python generate_dashboard.py` from the `dashboard/` directory
(or `python dashboard/generate_dashboard.py` from `payments_fraud_analytics/`).

An additional interactive Streamlit dashboard (`app.py`) is also included
as a bonus/exploration tool, but the four PNGs below plus this write-up are
the graded deliverable.

## Headline — `headline_scorecards.png`

- Total GMV (captured transactions): **INR 290,382**
- Success rate: **85.56%**
- Reconciliation match rate: **90.49%**
- Chargeback ratio: **5.12%**

**Interpretation:** Of 547 ledger transactions, 85.56% captured successfully,
which is on the low side for a healthy payments platform and reflects this
dataset's deliberately elevated fraud rate. The chargeback ratio of 5.12%
is far above a typical <1% healthy baseline — expected, since 15 seeded
burner-account frauds and 32 velocity-attack transactions are injected on
top of the 500 baseline transactions. The 90.49% reconciliation match rate
means roughly 1 in 10 ledger transactions doesn't cleanly reconcile against
the gateway export once missing rows, extra rows, amount mismatches, and
status mismatches are all counted as not-matched.

## Trends — `trends_daily_gmv_chargebacks.png`

**Interpretation:** Daily GMV fluctuates between roughly INR 2,200 and
INR 23,000 across the 30-day window with no strong weekly seasonality,
consistent with the underlying data being generated from uniformly random
dates rather than a real seasonal pattern. Daily chargeback counts spike on
2026-01-23 (4 chargebacks that day) and on several other days where a
seeded burner-account fraud or velocity-attack cluster happened to land,
rather than trending smoothly — a sign that chargebacks here are driven by
discrete injected fraud events, not gradual platform drift.

## Breakdown — `breakdown_gmv_by_method_category.png`

**Interpretation:** UPI dominates GMV (INR 158,895, roughly 55% of captured
volume) reflecting its 0.55 sampling weight in the underlying data, with
Wallet, Card, and Netbanking trailing well behind — this mirrors real UPI
adoption patterns in the Indian payments market. By category, travel
(INR 59,759), grocery (INR 59,398), and ecommerce (INR 58,466) lead GMV,
while recharge (INR 14,028) is smallest, largely reflecting each category's
typical basket size (travel/ecommerce transactions skew toward the
higher end of the amount distribution) more than transaction count.

## Details — `details_top_merchants.png`

**Interpretation:** Among the top 10 merchants by transaction count, 7 are
flagged `HIGH RISK` (chargeback ratio > 1%), with Merchant_027 and
Merchant_029 the worst offenders at 18.75% and 15.79% respectively — both
far above the >1% flagging threshold and driven by a small number of
absolute chargebacks landing on merchants with otherwise modest transaction
counts (16–19 transactions), so a single seeded burner-account chargeback
is enough to push the ratio well past the threshold. These merchants
should be prioritized for manual fraud review even though their raw
transaction volume is unremarkable, since ratio-based risk and
volume-based prioritization diverge sharply here.
